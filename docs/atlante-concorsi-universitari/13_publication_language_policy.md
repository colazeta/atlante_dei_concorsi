# 13 — Public-facing language policy

## Four-level statement model
1. **Documentary fact**: directly published in official/public documents.
2. **Descriptive indicator**: computed from documentary fields with explicit method.
3. **Review signal**: prioritisation cue for human review.
4. **Legal assessment**: outside scope; never published by this module.

## Allowed formulations
- `documented relationship`
- `profile with high descriptive specificity`
- `criteria with high degree of articulation`
- `potentially relevant relation for human review`
- `not determinable from available public documentation`

## Prohibited formulations
- `rigged`
- `corrupt`
- `conflict confirmed`
- `committee manipulated outcome`
- any wording implying liability or misconduct as established fact

## Neutral public wording examples
- `The procedure includes two published corrections and one committee appointment act.`
- `A documented relation of type coauthorship_single was recorded, with medium confidence.`
- `Criteria documentation is not determinable from available public documentation.`

## Internal-only wording examples
Use only for internal QA context, never public-facing:
- `manual review priority high due to missing key acts`
- `identity ambiguity unresolved`

## Rules for publishing committee-candidate documented relations
- Publish only relations with traceable evidence source.
- Include confidence level and relation type.
- Avoid interpretative or reputationally harmful extrapolation.
- Prefer summary-level publication (`publishable_summary`) unless review explicitly allows full release.

## Overstatement prevention rules
- Do not convert relation presence into misconduct claims.
- Distinguish explicitly between `documented relation` and `legal conflict`.
- Where evidence is weak, publish only uncertainty-aware wording or keep internal-only.
